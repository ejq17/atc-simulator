**Phrases audited: 151** (109 in sections A-F, 25 in section G (Tower vocabulary, rows 110-134) and 17 in section H (airport state, rows 135-151), both added 2026-10-18; rows in the tables below; the readback, pilot-call and ATIS functions were also run once to capture the exact strings the sim produces).

Verdicts: **109 phrases audited**. 82 correct as written; 13 rows carry an edit that was applied in files owned by this task (ATIS wording, lesson examples and explanations, FAA references); 14 rows need a change in `src/radio` / `src/sim` (exact strings in the section **For src/radio and src/sim**, ids R-F1, R-S1, R-S3, R-B1 to R-B7, R-I1).

## Decisions

* **Number pronunciation ("niner", "tree", "fife").** What the sources say: 7110.65 2-4-16 says "Use the ICAO pronunciation of numbers and individual letters" and TBL 2-4-1 lists `3 Three TREE`, `5 Five FIFE`, `9 Nine NIN-ER`, `0 Zero ZE-RO`; AIM 4-2-7 repeats the same table (`Three (TREE)`, `Five (FIFE)`, `Nine (NIN-ER)`, `Zero (ZEE-RO)`). The same documents spell every example with ordinary words ("Altimeter, three zero zero one", "one three thousand five hundred", AIM "Cessna Three One Six Zero Foxtrot") but write "niner" where the digit 9 is spoken ("One niner zero knots", "Flight Level One Niner Zero"). Decision: the simulator keeps "three" and "five" in its text, displays and parser vocabulary and uses "niner" for 9, because the table governs how a word is voiced, not how FAA text spells it, and only 9 is respelled in FAA text. If voiced "tree"/"fife" is wanted, do it in `src/voice/tts/prepareForTts.ts` (a TTS-only respelling), not in the strings. `src/commands/lexicon.ts` already maps `tree`, `fife`, `niner`, `fower`, `won`, `ate` to digits, so a controller who speaks ICAO-style is understood; no lexicon edit was needed.
* **Altitudes.** 2-4-17 requires separate digits for the thousands and the hundreds digit ("one seven thousand niner hundred"); group form ("ten thousand") is allowed only "for added clarity". The sim speaks "two thousand five hundred", "one zero thousand", "one two thousand five hundred", "flight level one eight zero", which is the required form.
* **Runway numbers.** Digit by digit, never padded: `Runway Three` (2-4-17). "runway four" is correct, "runway zero four" would not be.
* **Facility names.** "Atlantic City Tower" and "Atlantic City Approach" match the chart ("ATLANTIC CITY TOWER 120.3", "ATLANTIC CITY APP CON 124.6"); "Atlantic City Departure" matches AIM 4-2-6 and the DEP CON sectors on the ATLANTIC CITY THREE chart; Clearance Delivery must be "Clearance Delivery" (R-F1).
* **Handoff phrasing.** "Contact tower one two zero point three" is correct for KACY: 2-1-17 omits the location name when tower and TRACON share a name, and allows "Contact Departure" with or without the frequency.
* **Takeoff, landing, LUAW.** Runway first, then the instruction (3-9-4, 3-9-10, 3-10-5); all lessons and readbacks already do this.
* **Approach clearance.** "Cleared ILS runway one three approach" is the 4-8-1 / 5-9-4 form (type, runway, "approach"); the vector-and-clear lesson now uses the 5-9-4 wording "until established on the localizer".

## Ambiguities in the official sources

1. **TREE/FIFE written vs voiced** (above): the FAA tables give pronunciations, the examples use plain spelling. Decision recorded; reversible in the TTS layer.
2. **"Alfa/Juliett/Xray" vs "Alpha/Juliet/X-ray".** 2-4-16, AIM 4-2-7 and the PCG ATIS example write "Alfa", "Juliett", "Xray"; 7110.65 examples elsewhere write "Alpha", "Juliet", "X-RAY". Identical sound; the sim and parser accept both. Left as is.
3. **"Radar vectors" vs "then as filed" for ATLANTIC CITY THREE.** 4-3-2 lists "vector" as a route element and says SIDs with a radar-vector segment get "MAINTAIN (altitude)"; the 4-3-3 phraseology says "(SID) DEPARTURE, THEN AS FILED". The chart text ("for vectors to filed route. Maintain 2000, expect filed altitude ten (10) minutes after departure") supports either; the sim keeps "radar vectors" so the parser, readback and lesson agree.
4. **Minutes: "ten" or "one zero".** 4-3-3 examples say "one zero minutes after departure"; AIM 4-2-8 says every non-listed number is spoken digit by digit; common practice says "ten minutes". The sim now follows the 4-3-3 examples in the lesson (R-B2b for the readback).
5. **ATIS acknowledgement.** AIM 4-1-13 shows "Information Sierra received"; 2-9-2 shows the controller asking "Verify you have information CHARLIE". "With information Bravo" is not in either document but is universal in practice (R-B4 is therefore P3).
6. **ATIS update cadence.** 2-9-2 requires a new recording "upon receipt of any new official weather regardless of whether there is or is not a change in values". The manual does not define "official weather" in that paragraph; the sim takes the routine hourly METAR and a SPECI (not the 5-minute ASOS observations of the NWS feed). RESOLVED 2026-10-18 (row 83): a new official observation always makes a new letter; the material-change triggers are kept for everything between observations.
7. **Departure frequency at KACY.** The departure chart shows 124.6 (south sector) and 134.25 (north sector) "or as assigned"; 3-9-3 allows omitting the frequency when published on a SID. The sim always assigns 124.6.
8. **Frequency digits.** 2-4-17 limits controllers to two decimals (125.725 -> "...point seven two"); nothing in the AIM prescribes how pilots read back a three-decimal channel (R-S1 is optional).
9. **Runway pair ids.** Crossing calls use the id "13/31" and speak only the first end ("runway one three"). 3-7-2 examples name one designator; a pilot could be approaching from the 31 side. Harmless at KACY because the hold lines are on both sides of the same pavement and the controller's own instruction uses the same designator.
10. **"ATIS" voiced by TTS.** `src/voice/tts/prepareForTts.ts` spells ATIS letter by letter; the FAA says it as a word ("AY-tiss"). Outside the assigned files, noted for the voice owner.
